Everything on the wishlist

A loop from KL airport: fly straight on to Langkawi, then Penang, a night in Ipoh, and finish in Kuala Lumpur near the departure airport.

Adventure day

ESCAPE Penang on 1 Dec: ziplines, a zip coaster and the world's longest tube waterslide.

Resort beach

Four nights in Langkawi: Pantai Cenang sunsets, Tanjung Rhu, island hopping.

City nights

KL: Petronas Towers lit up, Saloma Link bridge, Jalan Alor, rooftop views.

Food

Everywhere, but Penang is the star. Full dish list below to tick off.

Day by day

Each day has a morning, afternoon and evening plan, how to get around, and a backup option for rain or tired legs.

Malacca is optional. Day 11 (Sun 6 Dec) has three plans: the Malacca day trip, Genting theme park, or an easy KL day. Pick one on the day's card; the rest of the trip stays the same.

    Getting around

    There's no Uber in Malaysia. Grab is the main ride app everywhere, trains link Penang, Ipoh and KL, and in KL you'll use a Touch 'n Go card.

    Grab

    Rides, food delivery and airport pickups in every stop. See the price before you book; pay by card in the app or cash.

    inDrive

    Alternative ride app in KL where you can offer a price. Handy when Grab surges.

    KTMB

    Official app and website for ETS train tickets (Penang, Ipoh, KL). Book ahead in December.

    Google Maps

    Train, bus and walking directions in KL and Penang, plus bus times.

    Arrival: KLIA to Langkawi

    Thu 26 Nov

    • Domestic flight, about 1 hour. Check which terminal your Langkawi flight leaves from: AirAsia uses KLIA Terminal 2, most other airlines Terminal 1.
    • Separate tickets: clear immigration, collect bags, check in again. Leave about 3 hours.
    • Langkawi airport to Pantai Cenang: Grab, about 10 to 15 minutes.

    On Langkawi

    26 to 30 Nov

    • Public transport is very limited. Use Grab, or rent a car for the day if you're doing several places.
    • Grab waits can be longer than in cities and prices jump at peak times. Book the airport and tour pickups early.
    • Renting a car: bring your licence (and an International Driving Permit to be safe), and check the rental company's rules.
    • Island hopping and mangrove tours usually include hotel or jetty pickup.

    Langkawi to Penang

    Mon 30 Nov

    • Short flight. The direct ferry is cancelled.
    • Backup: ferry to Kuala Kedah or Kuala Perlis, then bus or train. It takes most of a day.
    • Penang airport to George Town: Grab, about 30 to 40 minutes.

    In Penang

    30 Nov to 4 Dec

    • Grab is cheap and quick around George Town. The heritage core is walkable.
    • ESCAPE: Rapid Penang bus 101 from George Town to Teluk Bahang, or Grab.
    • Penang Hill and Kek Lok Si: Rapid Penang bus 204 goes to both, or Grab.

    Penang to Ipoh to KL

    4 and 5 Dec, ETS train

    • Get to Butterworth station from George Town by the ferry (the station is next to the terminal) or Grab across the bridge.
    • Butterworth to Ipoh is about 1.5 to 2 hours. Ipoh to KL Sentral about 2.5 hours, with many departures a day.
    • Book on the KTMB app or website. Gold class is fine; Platinum is roomier. Fares change with demand, so book early.
    • Ipoh old town is small: walk, or Grab to the cave temples.

    In Kuala Lumpur

    5 to 10 Dec

    • Buy a Touch 'n Go card at any Rapid KL station counter and top it up there. It works on MRT, LRT, Monorail and buses. Bank cards can't be tapped at the gates yet.
    • Riding a lot? The Rapid Kembara pass gives unlimited trains and buses: RM25 for 1 day or RM55 for 3 days, loaded on a Touch 'n Go card.
    • Batu Caves: KTM Komuter from KL Sentral, about 30 minutes, or Grab.
    • Thean Hou Temple and Putrajaya: easiest by Grab.
    • To the airport on 10 Dec: KLIA Ekspres from KL Sentral, about 30 minutes, RM55 (it goes up to RM65 in January 2027).

    Optional: KL to Malacca

    Sun 6 Dec

    • KTM Komuter from KL Sentral to Bandar Tasik Selatan (about 20 minutes), then walk to the TBS bus terminal.
    • Bus to Melaka Sentral: about 2.5 hours, every 15 to 30 minutes, from about RM12. Buy the return ticket ahead because Sunday buses fill up.
    • Melaka Sentral is a few km from the old town: take a Grab.
    • Door to door it's about 4 hours each way, so leave by 8:00.

    Optional: KL to Genting

    Alternative for Sun 6 Dec

    • Bus from KL Sentral or TBS to Awana, then the Awana SkyWay cable car up the mountain. About 1.5 hours total.
    • Or Grab all the way up for a group.
    • It's cool at the top. Bring a light jacket.

    Internet

    You need data for Grab and maps. Either buy a Malaysia eSIM before you fly and switch it on when you land, or buy a tourist SIM at the KLIA arrivals hall. Check your phone supports eSIM first.

    Cash or card?

    Both. Cards work in hotels, malls and restaurants; street food and night markets are mostly cash. The currency is the ringgit (RM). About RM4.06 to 1 US dollar (29 Oct 2026, mid-market rate).

    Card works for

    • Hotels, flights, train tickets and attraction tickets
    • Malls, supermarkets, chain cafes and sit-down restaurants
    • Grab, if you add your card in the app
    • Visa and Mastercard are widely accepted, contactless too. Above RM250 you'll usually need your PIN. Amex is less common.
    • Apple Pay or Google Wallet work where there's a contactless terminal, if your bank supports it.

    Bring cash for

    • Hawker stalls, night markets and Jonker Street
    • Small shops, street snacks, drinks
    • Topping up the Touch 'n Go card (counters take cards too)
    • Sarong rental at temples, small toilet fees, tips for guides
    • Many small places use QR codes (DuitNow) instead, which mostly need a Malaysian bank app. Cash is the easy fallback.

    Egyptian bank cards: check before you fly

    Egyptian banks limit foreign-currency spending, and the rules differ by bank.

    • Debit cards on Egyptian-pound accounts often don't work abroad at all. Cards linked to a USD account usually do.
    • Credit cards have a monthly foreign-spending cap. Many banks raise it if you activate a travel limit a few days before you leave, then ask for passport stamps afterwards.
    • ATM cash withdrawals abroad are capped low or blocked on many Egyptian cards.
    • Call your bank: ask your monthly foreign limit, how to activate travel mode, and whether ATM withdrawals in Malaysia are allowed.

    The money plan

    1. Activate the travel limit on your credit card before 26 Nov, and use it for hotels, flights and bigger bills.
    2. Bring US dollars in cash (clean, newer notes) as the main cash supply. Change them at licensed money changers in KL or Penang malls, which usually beat airport rates.
    3. Change a small amount at KLIA on arrival for the first day.
    4. Keep small notes on you every day for food stalls and markets.

    Before you go

    Entry

    Most current sources say Egyptian passports are visa-free for tourism, up to 90 days. One still says a visa is needed, so confirm on the official immigration site. Bring a passport valid 6 months past the trip and proof of your return flight. Fill in the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card online in the 3 days before landing.

    Book early

    Langkawi to Penang flight, ETS trains, and the Malacca return bus if you go. December school holidays make trains busy.

    Weather

    Hot and humid with afternoon showers. Do outdoor things in the morning, carry a small umbrella. The east coast islands (Perhentian, Redang, Tioman) are closed for the monsoon.

    What to wear

    Mosques and temples (Batu Caves, Putra Mosque, National Mosque) need shoulders and knees covered. Mosques lend robes. Pack light layers for air-conditioned malls and Genting or Penang Hill.
